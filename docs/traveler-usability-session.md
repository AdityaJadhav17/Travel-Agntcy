# Choosing and revisiting a flight

Run this task individually with five travelers. Give each person a fresh browser profile, a signed-in app, and the same search data. Include people who usually plan on a phone. Use a fixture search with at least two complete round trips, an overnight arrival, and an option with an unconfirmed return. Record which device each person uses.

## Read aloud

“Three adults are flying from Dallas to San Francisco for a round trip on the dates shown. Find a flight you would consider taking. Tell me what you would pay and what made you choose it. Then look into a different trip. Afterward, come back and show me the flight you chose.”

Supply the dates on a separate card. Let the traveler decide how to search and how to keep their choice. If they get stuck, ask “What did you expect to happen?” Record any assistance before giving it.

## Observe

| Moment | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Starting | First action, any hesitation, and whether the first submitted search matches the intended party and dates |
| Comparing | Which details they compare, whether they notice the arrival day and return status, and how they interpret the quoted total |
| Keeping a choice | Whether they find Save option unaided and whether they understand a saved fare can change |
| Returning | Whether they reopen the same itinerary after switching trips and reloading, how long it takes, and any wrong turns |

Afterward, ask “What would you still need before booking?” and “When, if ever, would you use this again?” Summarize the observed outcomes as counts out of five and keep participants’ explanations alongside them. Distinguish independent completion from completion with help. Treat a mistaken fare basis or an unconfirmed return mistaken for a complete trip as a decision error even if the task finishes.
